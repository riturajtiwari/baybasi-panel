# Fabrication

## Status: QA'd, routed, DRC-clean, Gerbers build

`export_fab.sh` produces the upload zip. `make_cpl.py` produces the JLCPCB
placement file and refuses to run if the BOM and the board disagree.

The routing is machine-generated by Freerouting (`./route.sh`) and re-runnable:
`gen_pcb.py` rebuilds the board from scratch, `route.sh` re-routes it. Neither
step needs the GUI.

    python3 gen_sch.py && kicad-cli sch export netlist -o net.net baybasi-ctrl.kicad_sch
    python3 gen_pcb.py         # placement, keepouts, stitching vias, silkscreen
    ./route.sh                 # DSN out, Freerouting, SES back, pad ties, pour repair, DRC
    ./export_fab.sh            # runs DRC, exports, zips - fails if unrouted
    <KiCad python3> make_cpl.py   # jlcpcb-cpl.csv from the board, cross-checked against jlcpcb-bom.csv

## Design QA, 2026-09-07

A pin-by-pin review of the schematic, the footprints and the layout against the
part datasheets. Four defects found and fixed, three hardening changes made.

| # | Finding | Severity | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Q1 was backwards.** Source faced the input, drain faced the load. A P-FET's body diode runs drain→source, so a reversed supply would have conducted straight through the load — the protection did nothing. | would have shipped a dead feature | Drain to J13 (input), source to L1 (load), gate to GND via R13. Correct polarity: diode conducts until Vgs = −5 V turns the channel on. Reversed: diode blocked, Vgs ≥ 0, channel off. |
| 2 | **/OE pull-up went to +5 V.** GPIO8 is not 5 V tolerant; with the devkit unpowered, 10 kΩ from 5 V back-drives its protection diode. | slow damage / boot flakiness | R15 to the board's own 3V3E rail. 3.3 V clears the AHCT VIH of 2.0 V. 3V3E rather than the devkit 3V3 so the outputs stay disabled with no devkit fitted. |
| 3 | **J1–J6 faced the wrong way.** Both Phoenix footprints put the wire opening on the +y face; on the top edge that pointed into the board. | unusable connectors | J1–J6 rotated 180°. J13 rotated 270° so the PSU leads enter from the left edge instead of running across Q1/L1/C1. |
| 4 | **Devkit body was mis-sized.** The outline was drawn symmetric about the pins; Espressif's footprint has 9.2 mm beyond the last pin at the USB end and 0.85 mm at the antenna end. The USB shells would have hung over the bottom resistor row. | mechanical | Socket moved up 3 mm; body box taken from the Espressif courtyard; silkscreen says which end is USB. |
| 5 | Twelve outputs float while /OE is high (boot, reset, no devkit). A floating WS2812B DIN reads noise as pixels. | cosmetic at power-up | R16–R27: 10 kΩ to GND on every OUT. |
| 6 | W5500 RSTn was pull-up only; firmware could not hard-reset the module. | robustness | RSTn also on GPIO15 (devkit pad 8). `ETH.begin(..., rst=15, ...)`. The pull-up stays. |
| 7 | Surface-mount GND pads relied on the pour reaching them; routing boxed one in. | routing | `route.sh` now ties every SMD GND pad to its own via before routing and repairs pour islands after the fill. |

Verified correct, no change: SPI on the S3's IOMUX pins (10–13) with INT on 14;
all twelve LED pins clear of strapping, USB, PSRAM and console pins; U1/U2
pinout (DIR high, A→B, unused A6/A7 grounded, OE on pin 19); SOT-223 tab = pin
2 = VOUT; W5500 module orientation (J1 left, J2 right, pin 1 at the RJ45 end,
RJ45 toward the top edge — WIZ820io UM figure, top view); female-socket rows
22.86 mm and 20.32 mm; AMS1117 dissipation 0.34 W; LC filter damped (Q ≈ 0.7);
1 A power traces at 1 mm.

Known, accepted: with the LED PSU off and USB plugged into the devkit, the
devkit's 5 V pin back-feeds this board and, through Q1's channel, the 5 V IN
terminal — so USB would try to power the panel column. Flash with the PSU on,
or with the panel supply disconnected. Confirm the devkit's own VBUS diode is
present (Espressif schematic, D1) before relying on the PSU-on case.

The auto-matcher picked SMD electrolytics for C1–C3 the first time; every
electrolytic above is the leaded ("插件") part and was chosen by hand.

**CPL conventions (make_cpl.py).** JLCPCB centres each part on the CPL
coordinate and uses its own zero orientation, so KiCad's position export is
wrong for anything whose anchor is pin 1. `make_cpl.py` reports the centre of
the pad bounding box and adds: SOT-223 +180, SOT-23 +180, DIP +270, pin
sockets +270, everything else +0. Verified against JLCPCB's placement overlay
for the parts above. Terminal-block entry direction is inferred from their
overlay arrows; confirm it on the placement image JLCPCB sends before
production ("Confirm Parts Placement" is on, auto-confirm off).

## Assembly notes

- JLCPCB places all SMD parts and the through-hole sockets, terminal blocks,
  inductor and electrolytics. You insert: the devkit, the W5500 module, and
  the two SN74AHCT245N into their DIP sockets.
- **DIP notch up** (toward the top edge) on U1 and U2 — pin 1 is top-left.
- **Devkit: USB ports toward the bottom edge**, 3V3 pin at the top-left of A1L.
- **W5500 module: RJ45 toward the top edge**, pin 1 (GND) at the top of each row.
- **J13:** square pad = +5 V. **J1–J12:** square pad = DATA, round = GND.
- J1–J6 wires leave over the top edge, J7–J12 over the bottom edge; the USB
  plug passes above J7–J9 with ~2.5 mm to spare (sockets 8.5 mm + header
  spacer 2.5 mm + devkit 1.6 mm).
- **Mount the board mid-column**, between panels 6 and 7. That keeps the longest
  data run near 4 ft instead of 8-10 ft, and puts the board's ground tap in the
  middle of the column's ground bus rather than at one end.
- Panel data leads are pre-made 3-pin JST-SM extension leads, 22 AWG, 2 m.
  White (GND) and green (DATA) land in J1-J12; the red +5 V conductor is cut and
  capped at **both** ends, because panel power never routes through this board.
- If one output is glitchy after everything else checks out, drop that channel's
  series resistor from 330 to 150 ohms. 330 over-damps a 2-3 m lead.

## Where to have them made

Board is 100 × 95 mm, 2-layer — 14.7 sq in. That size matters: shops that price
per square inch get expensive fast, while shops with a flat "under 100 × 100 mm"
tier are at their cheapest.

| Option | Build | In your hands | ~Cost, 10 boards | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **JLCPCB + assembly**, DHL express | 3–5 days | **~1 week** | ~$60–90 boards + parts | Economic PCBA, top side. Best cost/speed balance. |
| PCBWay + assembly | similar | ~1–2 weeks | somewhat more | Equivalent; use if JLC's DFM queue stalls |
| Bay Area Circuits (Fremont), bare boards | same day | same/next day | $$$ | Only if the schedule turns on days; you then place ~50 SMD parts per board yourself |

**Recommendation: JLCPCB with assembly and express shipping.**

One caveat on the "24 hour build" everywhere: the clock starts *after* DFM
review, which can add a day. Submit early in their working day and check for
DFM queries so the file is not sitting waiting on your reply.

## Before you click order

- [ ] `./export_fab.sh` exits 0 — no unrouted nets, no DRC errors
- [ ] `python3 make_cpl.py` says "BOM and CPL agree"
- [ ] **Print the layout at 1:1 on paper and stand the real parts on it.** Every
      one, including the devkit and the W5500. Check the printer did not scale.
- [ ] RJ45 face sits 6 mm inside the top edge; the plug clears H1's standoff
- [ ] Every part JLCPCB matched is the intended value and package (3.5 mm
      blocks for J1–J12, 5.08 mm for J13, DIP-20 *socket* for U1/U2)
- [ ] Placement preview: SOT-223 tab up, SOT-23 pin 1 where the silkscreen dot is
- [ ] Gerber preview on the fab's site looks like `board3d.png`
