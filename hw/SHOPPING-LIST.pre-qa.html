Two things worth knowing before you spend

Do not pay the WIZnet premium. DigiKey want $24.52 for one WIZ850io and $19.68 each in tens — about $197 for the Ethernet alone. The W5500 chip inside costs a few dollars; the rest is distributor margin on a low-volume first-party module. The USR-ES1 clone is the same silicon on the same 23 × 25 mm board, specified pin-to-pin compatible, at roughly a third of the price. The USR-ES1 / W5500 Lite is specified as pin-to-pin compatible with the WIZ850io on an identical 23 × 25 mm board, so it inherits the 20.32 mm spacing. It is the same W5500 silicon at roughly a third of the price.

Do not substitute the WIZ820io. It is mechanically identical and sold alongside the WIZ850io, but it carries a W5200, not a W5500. Different register map, no driver in the ESP32 Ethernet stack we use, and it needs a hardware reset pulse our board does not generate. It is also end-of-life.

Rough total, excluding LED power supplies: about $340 in board parts with clone Ethernet modules (about $470 if you insist on WIZnet), plus about $135 for the second Pi, two switches and cabling, plus about $30 of PCBs. Call it $510. Add US tariffs — DigiKey warn they may apply and are only shown at checkout.

Three things that will bite if you get them wrong

1. AHCT, not HC. An SN74HC245 looks identical and will not reliably read 3.3 V as a logic high from a 5 V rail.

2. Terminal block pitch. 3.5 mm and 3.81 mm are sold side by side and look the same in a photograph. The board is drilled for 3.5.

3. The W5500 module is 3.3 V only. It has no regulator. 5 V on its V pins destroys it instantly. The larger blue W5500 boards do take 5 V — do not mix them up.

Power supplies for the LEDs are excluded — you said those are handled separately. For reference the design target is roughly 295 A per display at 5 V, sized at 40% of the 737 A theoretical maximum.
